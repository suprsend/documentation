Analytics provides visibility into your notification system. Track notification lifecycle signals, understand user engagement patterns, and optimize your workflows based on near real-time data.

Navigate to the [Analytics tab](https://app.suprsend.com/en/production/analytics/) to get started.

<Note>
  **Analytics vs Logs:** Analytics shows trends and summaries (like "email has 45% open rate"). [Logs](/docs/logging) shows individual events (like "this email was delivered at 2:34 PM"). Use Analytics to spot patterns, then use Logs to investigate specific issues.
</Note>

---

## Overview

Analytics helps you answer three questions:

1. **Are notifications being delivered?**
2. **Are users engaging?**
3. **Are users opting out—and why?**

## Performance Overview

Performance Overview shows delivery, engagement (where supported), volume, and opt-outs across channels.

### Channel performance

Compare delivery and engagement across channels to see where users are most responsive.

<Frame caption="Which channels are performing the best?">
  ![](/images/channel-perf.png)
</Frame>

**Core metrics:**

| Metric | What it means | How it's computed | Why it matters |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Delivered** | Provider reports a delivered (or final) status | Recorded when SuprSend receives a delivery confirmation (or equivalent status) from the provider | If delivery is low, notifications aren't reaching recipients reliably |
| **Seen** | A view/open signal is received (varies by channel) | Recorded when tracking events are received (see [Setting up tracking](#setting-up-tracking)) | Shows if your content is engaging enough to open |
| **Clicked** | User clicked on the notification | Tracked when users click links or buttons in notifications via click tracking URLs | Indicates if your call-to-action is effective |
| **Opt-out** | The user unsubscribed from notifications | Tracked when users unsubscribe via links, preferences, or API | High opt-outs mean users are annoyed or notifications aren't relevant |

<Note>
  For Android push, "seen" also includes notifications cleared from the tray. "Seen" and "Clicked" metrics require tracking setup. Some channels (SMS, Slack) don't support these metrics.
</Note>

**Benchmarks:**

| Metric | ✅ Good | ⚠️ Warning | 🔴 Bad |
|--------|---------|------------|--------|
| **Delivery rate** | 95%+ | 85–95% | Below 85% |
| **Email open rate** | 20%+ | 10–20% | Below 10% |
| **Push seen rate** | 40%+ | 20–40% | Below 20% |
| **WhatsApp seen rate** | 60%+ | 40–60% | Below 40% |
| **Click rate** | 3%+ | 1–3% | Below 1% |
| **Opt-out rate** | Below 0.1% | 0.1–0.5% | Above 0.5% |

<Note>
  These are general benchmarks. Benchmarks vary by industry, use case, and user base. Focus on trends over time—are your metrics improving or declining?
</Note>

**Channel-level engagement trends:** View delivery, seen, click, and volume trends over time for each channel to identify optimal send times and compare performance.

**How to interpret:**

- **High delivery + High engagement** → Channel is working well. Consider increasing volume or using this channel for priority notifications.
- **High delivery + Low engagement** → Users receive notifications but aren't opening them. Review content, timing, or relevance.
- **Low delivery** → Check vendor issues, invalid addresses, or delivery failures in the Errors tab.
- **Low engagement** → Review content quality, send times, or if the channel matches your audience preferences.

Use filters and exports to drill down into specific data points. For export schema and columns, see [Exporting data](#exporting-data).

**What to look for:**

- **Outlier channel (best/worst)** → One channel significantly outperforms or underperforms others
- **Engagement by time** → Engagement varies by time of day or day of week
- **Delivery drop** → Sudden decrease in delivery rate (check vendor issues or data problems)
- **Rising opt-outs** → Opt-out rate increasing (check volume or relevance)

<Warning>
  **Warning sign: Tracking not properly set up**

  If you see "seen" or "clicked" metrics showing as 0% or missing for channels that support them (email, push, WhatsApp), tracking isn't configured. Configure `https://hub.suprsend.com/webhook/*` in your vendor account. See [vendor integration docs](/docs/vendors).
</Warning>

### Notification volume

Monitor average notifications sent per user per day. **Over-messaging is the #1 reason users unsubscribe.**

<Frame caption="Average notifications per user showing volume trends and correlation with opt-outs">
  ![](/images/avgnotif.png)
</Frame>

**What you'll see:**

- **Average notifications per user** — Average number of notifications each user receives per day
- **Volume trends over time** — How notification volume changes over time
- **Volume by category** — Which notification categories contribute most to total volume
- **Volume by channel** — Which channels are sending the most notifications
- **Correlation with opt-outs** — Compare volume trends and opt-out trends side by side to check if spikes align

| Status | Volume | What it means |
|--------|--------|--------------|
| ✅ **Good** | 2-3 notifications per user per day | Users are engaged without being overwhelmed |
| ⚠️ **Warning** | 4-9 per day | Monitor opt-out rates closely |
| 🔴 **Red zone** | 10+ per day | Users will likely unsubscribe. Take action immediately. |

<Note>
  These ranges are general guidelines. Transaction-heavy products may legitimately exceed them. Monitor your opt-out rates to determine what works for your users.
</Note>

<Warning>
  **If you're sending 10+ per day (red zone):** High risk of opt-outs and reduced engagement. **Recommended: 2–3 notifications per user per day.** Filter by category to find which notifications are driving high volume, then reduce frequency or batch notifications together.
</Warning>

**How to investigate:**

1. Check if notification volume correlates with rising opt-outs
2. Filter by category to find which category sends the most (often marketing/promotional)
3. Check Workflow Level Breakdown—sort by count to find high-volume workflows
4. Review timing—are multiple notifications sent at the same time?

**How to reduce volume:**

- Use [throttling](/docs/throttle) to limit how often workflows trigger per user
- Use [digest](/docs/digest) to batch multiple updates into one notification
- Review workflow triggers—are conditions too broad?
- Prioritize—send only critical notifications immediately; batch others

Monitor opt-outs by channel, category, and time period.

**What you'll see:**

- **Total opt-outs** — Number and percentage of users who unsubscribed

- **Category breakdown** — Which categories drive the most opt-outs

- **Correlation with volume** — Compare opt-out trends and volume trends side by side to check if spikes align

<Note>
  This granular tracking is linked to [user preferences](/docs/user-preferences).

**When to worry:**

<Warning>
  Watch out for: Opt-out rate above 0.5%, spike in opt-outs for a specific category, high opt-outs from one channel, opt-out rate increasing over time
</Warning>

**How to investigate:**

1. **Check notification volume first** — Most common cause is too many notifications. Check if volume correlates with opt-out trends.
2. **Filter by category** — Which categories have highest opt-out rates?
3. **Filter by channel** — Which channels are driving opt-outs?
4. **Check day-wise trends** — Did opt-outs spike after a specific campaign?
5. **Review content** — Are notifications relevant and valuable?

## Workflow Level Breakdown

Compare performance across workflows, templates, channels, vendors, and categories side by side.

<Frame caption="Workflow Level Breakdown view">
  ![](/images/wf-level.png)
</Frame>

### Understanding the table

Each row represents one unique combination of workflow + delivery path (template, channel, vendor, category) with performance metrics.

**Columns:** Workflow, Template, Channel, Vendor, Category, Count, Delivery rate, Seen rate, Click rate

### Filters and sorting

**Filters:** Workflow, channel, category, vendor, template, **interaction status (good/bad)**

**Interaction filters:**
- **Good interactions** — Show only workflows with high engagement (high seen/click rates)
- **Bad interactions** — Show only workflows with low engagement (low seen/click rates)

**Sorting:** Click any column header to sort the table:
- Find underperformers → Sort by lowest seen/click rate
- Find top performers → Sort by highest seen/click rate
- Find high-volume workflows → Sort by count

<Tip>
  **Pro tip:** Combine filters to drill down. Example: "Show me all email workflows in marketing category sent via SendGrid with good interactions."
</Tip>

<Note>
  **Using dynamic workflows?** Don't mix parameters between dynamic and non-dynamic workflows. See [dynamic workflow API docs](/docs/managing-notification-categories#in-dynamic-workflow-api) for details on data types and parameter usage.
</Note>

### Common scenarios

- **Find your best workflows** → Sort by click rate (highest first), identify top performers, analyze what makes them successful
- **Fix underperformers** → Filter by "bad interactions" or sort by lowest seen/click rate, compare with high-performing workflows, test improvements
- **Compare similar workflows** → Filter by category (e.g., "transactional"), compare workflows in the same category, optimize underperformers
- **Optimize channels** → Filter by channel (e.g., "email"), sort by engagement metrics, consider using [smart delivery](/docs/smart-delivery)

Use filters and exports to drill down into specific workflows. Export raw data for user-level analysis.

## Errors

The Errors tab aggregates failures across workflows and vendors to help you prioritize fixes. Unlike detailed logs (which can be overwhelming), this shows error occurrence count and last seen time, making it easy to identify patterns.

<Frame caption="Errors view">
  ![](/images/error.png)
</Frame>

### Error summary (Failure log)

**What you'll see:**
- **Occurrence count** — How many times each error happened (prioritize high counts)
- **Last seen** — When the error was last observed (identify if it's ongoing or resolved)
- **Error type** — API Request Failures, Workflow Execution Failures, or Notification Delivery Failures
- **Error message** — What went wrong (with specific details)

**Why use Errors tab instead of logs:** Logs show every single event (overwhelming). Errors tab shows aggregated information (easy to spot patterns).

**How to use it:**

1. **Check occurrence count** → High counts need immediate attention
2. **Check last seen** → If not seen recently, might already be fixed
3. **Filter by error type** → Focus on what you're investigating
4. **Filter by workflow/vendor** → Identify if errors are specific to certain workflows or vendors

### API Request Failures

Requests failed before workflows start.

**Common causes:** Invalid API keys, wrong request format, authentication issues, missing required fields

**What to do:**

1. Check API keys in your code
2. Verify request format matches API documentation
3. Review recent code changes
4. Check if you're using the correct workspace (staging vs production)

**If you see "No API error found":** If you're actively making requests, your integration is working. Any API/SDK issues will appear here.

### Workflow / Broadcast Execution Failures

Errors during workflow execution (often template rendering errors).

**Common error: Template rendering failures**

Missing variables in templates:
```
email: template rendering failed. error: "code" not defined in [object Object] - 72:20
email: template rendering failed. error: "actor_name" not defined in [object Object] - 43:30
```

**What to do:**

1. Check the [templates](/docs/templates) for the affected workflow
2. Verify all required variables are in your data payload
3. Review [workflow configuration](/docs/workflow-builder) to ensure data is passed correctly
4. The error message tells you exactly which variable is missing

**Other common causes:** Invalid data types, missing required fields, data validation errors

**How to debug:**

1. Filter by workflow to see all errors for that workflow
2. Check the error message—it tells you what's missing or wrong
3. Review your data payload to ensure all required variables are included
4. See [Error Guide](/docs/error-guides) for detailed troubleshooting

### Notification Delivery Failures

Errors reported by vendors for failed deliveries. These happen after SuprSend successfully sends the request to the vendor, but the vendor can't deliver.

**Common errors:**

**Invalid email/phone addresses:**
```
Invalid
550 Requested action not taken: mailbox unavailable
550 5.1.1 The email account that you tried to reach does not exist
```

**What to do:**

1. Update user data with valid email addresses or phone numbers
2. Verify contact information before sending
3. Remove invalid addresses from your user database

**Vendor bounces:** Usually invalid addresses → Update user data. Some bounces are temporary (mailbox full) → Vendor will retry automatically.

**How to debug:**

1. Filter by vendor to see if it's vendor-specific
2. Filter by channel to see if it's channel-specific
3. Check vendor status page for reported outages
4. See [Error Guide](/docs/error-guides) for vendor-specific error resolution

### Can you fix it?

Not all delivery failures are fixable:

| Error Type | Can you fix it? | What to do |
|------------|----------------|------------|
| **Invalid email/phone number** | ✅ Yes | Update user data, verify contact information |
| **Vendor outage** | ❌ No (temporary) | Wait for vendor to resolve, or switch vendors |
| **Vendor rate limiting** | ✅ Yes | Adjust sending frequency, contact vendor for higher limits |
| **Blocked by vendor** | ✅ Yes | Check vendor dashboard, resolve reputation issues |
| **Template rendering error** | ✅ Yes | Fix template syntax, verify required variables |
| **Mailbox unavailable** | ⚠️ Sometimes | Usually invalid address, but could be temporary (mailbox full) |
| **Email account does not exist** | ✅ Yes | Update user data with valid email addresses |

<Note>
  **Errors vs Logs:** Use the Errors tab to quickly spot issues and see patterns (occurrence count, last seen). Use [**Logs**](/docs/logging) when you need detailed debugging information for a specific failure.
</Note>

## Exporting data

Export CSVs for analysis outside SuprSend (Sheets/Excel/warehouse).

<Frame caption="Export analytics dialog">
  ![](/images/export-analytics.png)
</Frame>

### How to export

1. Navigate to the Analytics tab you want to export
2. Click the three dots (⋯) in the top right of that section
3. Click "Export as CSV"
4. Select your date range (relative or absolute)
5. Choose your export type (aggregate or raw user data)
6. Apply any filters you want to include
7. Download the CSV file(s)

### Export types

**Aggregate user data** — Summary statistics grouped by date and time (matches what you see in charts). Includes: Date/time buckets, total counts, percentage rates, grouped by workflow/channel/category/vendor/template. Use cases: Custom dashboards, trend analysis, high-level reporting.

**Raw user data** — Individual user-level records with timestamps for available events. Includes: User records, timestamps for each event, workflow/template/channel/vendor/category, user properties and metadata. Use cases: Detailed user behavior analysis, pattern identification, custom analytics, data warehousing.

### Export limits

**File size limit:** Up to 50 million rows per file

**Multiple files:** If your export exceeds 50M rows, SuprSend automatically splits it into multiple files at the graph level.

**Partition ID:** Each file includes a `partition_id` column (0, 1, 2...) to identify and merge files. The partition ID tells you the file number:
- **1 file** → `partition_id = 0`
- **2 files** → `partition_id = 0` (first file), `partition_id = 1` (second file)
- **3 files** → `partition_id = 0, 1, 2` (and so on)

**How to merge multiple files:**

1. Download all files from your export
2. Sort by `partition_id` to ensure correct order
3. Combine files in order (partition_id 0, then 1, then 2, etc.)
4. Remove duplicate headers (keep header only from first file)

## Frequently Asked Questions

<AccordionGroup>

  <Accordion title="How up-to-date is the data?">
    Data updates in near real-time, usually within a few minutes. For the most recent hour, data might still be updating (expect some variance). For events from 1-24 hours ago, most data is in. After 48 hours, data is final and won't change. For immediate verification of a specific event, use [Logs](/docs/logging).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What timezone is the data shown in?">
    Analytics use your browser's timezone by default. You can change it in the top navbar if needed.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How long is data kept?">
    Data retention depends on your plan. Check the [pricing page](https://www.suprsend.com/pricing) for details.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What are good benchmark metrics for my industry?">
    See the [Benchmarks](#channel-performance) section above. Benchmarks vary by industry, use case, and channel. Focus on trends over time—are your metrics improving?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How often should I check analytics?">
    Daily for errors, weekly for engagement trends, monthly for deep optimization.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Seen/clicked metrics are missing">
    Tracking not configured, vendor not sending engagement events, or recent notifications (data may still be updating). See [Setting up tracking](#setting-up-tracking). Note: Some channels (SMS, Slack) don't support seen/clicked metrics.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Opt-out rate is high">
    See the [Unsubscription trends](#unsubscription-trends) section above for investigation steps and how to reduce volume.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Numbers keep changing">
    Why this happens: Vendors send status updates over time. Recent data (last 24-48 hours) is still updating. International SMS vendors are often slower to report. What to know: Data from last 24 hours may still be updating, data from 24-48 hours ago is mostly final, data older than 48 hours is final and won't change. For immediate verification, use [Logs](/docs/logging).
  </Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>
