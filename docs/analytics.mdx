Analytics provides visibility into your notification system. Track notification lifecycle signals, understand user engagement patterns, and optimize your workflows based on near real-time data.

Navigate to the [Analytics tab](https://app.suprsend.com/en/production/analytics/) to get started.

<Note>
  **Analytics vs Logs:** Analytics shows trends and summaries (like "email has 45% open rate"). [Logs](/docs/logging) shows individual events (like "this email was delivered at 2:34 PM"). Use Analytics to spot patterns, then use Logs to investigate specific issues.
</Note>

## Performance Overview

Performance Overview provides a high-level view of your notification system's health. Start here to assess overall performance, then explore specific areas in detail.

### Channel performance

Compare delivery and engagement across channels to see where users are most responsive.

<Frame caption="Which channels are performing the best?">
  ![](/images/channel-perf.png)
</Frame>

**Filters:** Filter by template to analyze performance for specific templates across channels.

**Core metrics:**

| Metric | What it means | How it's computed | Why it matters |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Delivered** | Provider or channel reports a delivered (or final) status | Recorded when SuprSend receives a provider delivery status (or equivalent final status) | If delivery is low, notifications aren't reaching recipients reliably |
| **Seen** | A view/open signal is received (varies by channel) | Recorded when tracking events are received | Shows if your content is engaging enough to open |
| **Clicked** | User clicked on the notification | Tracked when users click links or buttons in notifications via click tracking URLs | Indicates if your call-to-action is effective |
| **Opt-out** | The user unsubscribed from notifications | Tracked when users unsubscribe via links, preferences, or API | High opt-outs mean users are annoyed or notifications aren't relevant |

**Channel support for metrics:**

Not all channels support all metrics. Here's what's available at the channel level:

| Channel | Delivered | Seen | Clicked |
|---------|-----------|------|---------|
| Email | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| SMS | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |
| WhatsApp | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ |
| Android Push | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| iOS Push | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Web Push | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Inbox | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Slack | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |
| MS Teams | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |

<Note>
  For Android push, "seen" also includes notifications cleared from the tray. "Seen" and "Clicked" metrics require tracking setup. Some channels (SMS, Slack) don't support these metrics.
</Note>

**Benchmarks:**

Use these benchmarks as a starting point, but remember they vary by industry, use case, and user base. Focus on trends over time—are your metrics improving or declining?

| Metric | ✅ Good | ⚠️ Warning | 🔴 Bad |
|--------|---------|------------|--------|
| **Delivery rate** | 95%+ | 85–95% | Below 85% |
| **Email open rate** | 20%+ | 10–20% | Below 10% |
| **Push seen rate** | 40%+ | 20–40% | Below 20% |
| **WhatsApp seen rate** | 60%+ | 40–60% | Below 40% |
| **Click rate** | 3%+ | 1–3% | Below 1% |
| **Opt-out rate** | Below 0.1% | 0.1–0.5% | Above 0.5% |

<Note>
  Inbox metrics vary significantly depending on whether Inbox is used as a primary or secondary notification channel.
</Note>

**Interpreting your metrics:**

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Low delivery rates" icon="exclamation-triangle" iconType="solid">
    Investigate vendor issues, invalid contact information, or delivery failures in [Errors](#errors).
  </Card>
  <Card title="Low engagement rates" icon="question-circle" iconType="solid">
    Review content and send timing. Use [Workflow Level Breakdown](#workflow-level-breakdown) to find which workflows are dragging the metric. If volume is high, use [batching](/docs/batch) or [throttling](/docs/throttle).
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

**Key patterns to watch:**

- **Outlier channels** → Identify channels with consistently high or low performance. Use [Workflow Level Breakdown](#workflow-level-breakdown) to compare workflows and understand which specific workflows drive these patterns.

- **Time-based engagement patterns** → Engagement often varies by time of day or day of week. Use time-based filters to identify optimal send times and compare engagement rates across different periods.

- **Sudden delivery drops** → A sudden decrease in delivery rate indicates potential issues. Check [Errors](#errors) and see the [Error Guide](/docs/error-guides) for detailed troubleshooting.

For export options and schema details, see [Exporting data](#exporting-data).

### Notification volume

Monitor average notifications sent per user per day. **Over-messaging is the #1 reason users unsubscribe.**

<Note>
  **Most useful when you've set up notification categories:** This section is most useful when you have [notification categories](/docs/managing-notification-categories) set up, as it allows you to break down volume by category.
</Note>

<Frame caption="Average notifications per user showing volume trends and correlation with opt-outs">
  ![](/images/avgnotif.png)
</Frame>

**What you'll see:**

- **Average notifications per user** — Average number of notifications each user receives per day
- **Volume trends over time** — How notification volume changes over time
- **Volume by category** — Which notification categories contribute most to total volume
- **Volume by channel** — Which channels are sending the most notifications
- **Correlation with opt-outs** — Compare volume trends and opt-out trends side by side to check if spikes align

| Status | Volume | What it means |
|--------|--------|--------------|
| ✅ **Good** | 2-3 notifications per user per day | Users are engaged without being overwhelmed |
| ⚠️ **Warning** | 4-9 per day | Monitor opt-out rates closely |
| 🔴 **Red zone** | 10+ per day | Users will likely unsubscribe. Take action immediately. |

<Note>
  These ranges are general guidelines. Transaction-heavy products may legitimately exceed them. Monitor your opt-out rates to determine what works for your users.
</Note>

**Investigation steps:**

1. Check if notification volume trends correlate with rising opt-outs
2. Filter by category to identify which categories send the most notifications
3. Use [Workflow Level Breakdown](#workflow-level-breakdown)—sort by trigger count to find high-volume workflows
4. Review timing patterns—are multiple notifications being sent simultaneously?

See [Unsubscription trends](#unsubscription-trends) for detailed opt-out investigation steps.

**Reducing volume:**

- Use [throttling](/docs/throttle) to limit how often workflows trigger per user
- Use [batch](/docs/batch) to consolidate multiple updates into single notifications
- Review workflow triggers—ensure conditions aren't too broad
- Prioritize notifications—send critical ones immediately and batch others

### Unsubscription trends

Monitor opt-outs by channel, category, and time period to understand why users unsubscribe.

<Note>
  **User preferences required:** This section is available when you've enabled SuprSend's [preference center](/docs/user-preferences). Without preferences, opt-outs may be limited or unavailable. With preferences enabled, you can track opt-outs by category, channel, and time period to identify patterns and understand why users unsubscribe.
</Note>

<Frame caption="Unsubscription trends showing opt-out patterns, day-wise breakdown, and category/channel analysis">
  ![](/images/optout.png)
</Frame>

**What you'll see:**

- **Total opt-outs** — Number and percentage of users who unsubscribed
- **Day-wise trends** — Opt-out patterns over time (use available time breakdowns to spot spikes)
- **Category breakdown** — Which categories drive the most opt-outs
- **Channel breakdown** — Which channels contribute most to opt-outs
- **Opt-out rate** — Percentage of users who unsubscribed
- **Correlation with volume** — Compare opt-out trends and volume trends side by side to check if spikes align

**Warning signs:**

<Warning>
  Watch for: Opt-out rate above 0.5%, spikes in opt-outs for specific categories, high opt-outs from a single channel, or opt-out rates increasing over time
</Warning>

**Investigation steps:**

1. **Check notification volume first** — Over-messaging is the most common cause. Check if volume trends correlate with opt-out spikes.
2. **Filter by category** — Identify which categories have the highest opt-out rates.
3. **Filter by channel** — Determine which channels are driving opt-outs.
4. **Analyze day-wise trends** — Check if opt-outs spiked after a specific campaign or change.
5. **Review content relevance** — Assess whether notifications are valuable and relevant to users.

## Workflow Level Breakdown

After identifying issues or opportunities in Performance Overview, use Workflow Level Breakdown to compare specific workflows, templates, channels, and vendors.

Workflow Level Breakdown provides a unified view to compare performance across workflows, templates, channels, vendors, and categories. Use this to identify top performers, workflows that need optimization, and how different delivery paths (template + channel + vendor combinations) affect engagement.

<Frame caption="Workflow Level Breakdown view">
  ![](/images/wf-level.png)
</Frame>

### How it works

Each row in the table represents a unique combination of:
- **Workflow** + **Template** + **Channel** + **Vendor** + **Category**

Along with performance metrics: **Trigger count**, **Delivery rate**, **Seen rate**, and **Click rate**.

### Using filters and sorting

**Available filters:**
- **Workflow** — Filter by specific workflows
- **Channel** — Compare performance across channels
- **Category** — Analyze by notification category
- **Vendor** — Compare vendor performance
- **Template** — See which templates perform best
- **Interaction status** — Filter by "good interactions" (high engagement) or "bad interactions" (low engagement)

**Sorting:**
Click any column header to sort by that column. Common use cases:
- **Find underperformers** → Sort by lowest seen/click rate
- **Find top performers** → Sort by highest seen/click rate  
- **Find high-volume workflows** → Sort by trigger count (highest first)

You can sort by any column in the table: Workflow, Template, Channel, Vendor, Category, Trigger count, Delivery rate, Seen rate, or Click rate.

<Tip>
  **Pro tip:** Combine multiple filters for precise analysis. For example: "Show me all email workflows in the marketing category sent via SendGrid with good interactions" to identify your best-performing marketing emails.
</Tip>

### Common use cases

**1. Identify and replicate top performers**

Find your best-performing workflows and understand what makes them successful:

- **Sort by click rate (highest first)** to surface workflows with the highest engagement
- **Filter by category** to compare workflows within the same category (e.g., all transactional emails)
- **Analyze success factors:** Review template design, channel selection, send timing, and content that drives engagement
- **Apply learnings:** Replicate successful patterns (template structure, channel choice, timing) to underperforming workflows in similar categories

**2. Diagnose and fix underperforming workflows**

Quickly identify workflows that need attention and prioritize fixes:

- **Filter by "bad interactions"** or **sort by lowest seen/click rate** to find workflows with low engagement
- **Compare with top performers** in the same category to identify gaps
- **Drill down by template, channel, or vendor** to pinpoint specific issues (e.g., one template performs poorly while others excel)
- **Test improvements:** Apply strategies from high-performing workflows and measure impact

**3. Compare workflows within categories**

Understand performance differences between similar workflows:

- **Filter by category** (e.g., "transactional", "promotional", "alerts") to isolate workflows serving similar purposes
- **Compare side by side:** Look at delivery rates, engagement metrics, and trigger counts to identify optimization opportunities
- **Identify patterns:** Determine if certain templates, channels, or vendors consistently perform better within a category
- **Optimize systematically:** Focus improvements on workflows that deviate significantly from category benchmarks

**4. Optimize channel selection and strategy**

Determine which channels work best for different types of notifications:

- **Filter by channel** (e.g., "email", "push", "SMS") to see all workflows using that channel
- **Sort by engagement metrics** (seen rate, click rate) to identify which workflows perform best on each channel
- **Compare channels:** Use the same workflow across different channels to see which channel drives better engagement
- **Automate selection:** Consider using [smart delivery](/docs/smart-delivery) to automatically route notifications to the best-performing channel per user

**5. Evaluate and optimize vendor performance**

Compare vendor reliability and engagement to optimize your vendor mix:

- **Filter by vendor** to see delivery rates and engagement metrics for each vendor
- **Compare delivery rates:** Identify vendors with consistently high or low delivery rates
- **Analyze by channel:** Some vendors may perform better for specific channels (e.g., Vendor A excels for email, Vendor B for SMS)
- **Optimize routing:** Use vendor performance data to route critical notifications through your most reliable vendors

**Exporting data:** Download raw data for deeper analysis and to identify optimization opportunities.

<Note>
  **Multi-tenant analytics:** If you're using multi-tenant setups, look for tenant filters (where available) to isolate metrics per tenant.
</Note>

## Errors

When you notice issues in Performance Overview (like low delivery rates), use Errors to identify and fix the root causes. Errors aggregates failures across your notification system, showing error occurrence count and last seen time to help you spot patterns and prioritize fixes.

<Frame caption="Errors view showing the Failure log">
  ![](/images/error.png)
</Frame>

### Understanding error information

Each error shows:
- **Occurrence count** — How many times the error happened (prioritize high counts)
- **Last seen** — When the error was last observed (identify if it's ongoing or resolved)
- **Error type** — API Request Failures, Workflow Execution Failures, or Notification Delivery Failures
- **Error message** — Specific details about what went wrong

**Using Errors:**
1. **Prioritize by occurrence count** → High counts require immediate attention
2. **Check last seen timestamp** → If not seen recently, the issue may already be resolved
3. **Filter by error type** → Focus your investigation on specific error categories
4. **Filter by workflow/vendor** → Determine if errors are isolated to specific workflows or vendors

### API Request Failures

These errors occur when requests fail before workflows start executing, indicating SuprSend couldn't process your API request. Errors due to which API requests were not successfully completed.

**Example errors:**
- Invalid API key
- Authentication failed
- Missing required field: user_id
- Invalid request format

**Filters:** API type, error description

**Sorting:** Sort by any column to prioritize errors by occurrence count, last seen time, or error type.

For detailed troubleshooting and resolution steps, see the [Error Guide](/docs/error-guides).

### Workflow / Broadcast Execution Failures

These errors occur during workflow execution, often due to template rendering issues or data validation problems. Errors encountered during workflow or broadcast run due to which one or more nodes in the flow would have failed.

**Example errors:**
```
email: template rendering failed. error: "code" not defined in [object Object] - 72:20
email: template rendering failed. error: "actor_name" not defined in [object Object] - 43:30
```

**Filters:** Node name, error description

**Sorting:** Sort by any column to prioritize errors by occurrence count, last seen time, or error type.

For detailed troubleshooting and resolution steps, see the [Error Guide](/docs/error-guides).

### Notification Delivery Failures

These errors occur after SuprSend successfully sends the request to the vendor, but the vendor cannot deliver the notification. The vendor reports the failure back to SuprSend. Errors reported by vendors for failed notification deliveries.

**Example errors:**
```
550 Requested action not taken: mailbox unavailable
550 5.1.1 The email account that you tried to reach does not exist
Invalid phone number
```

**Filters:** Template slug, channel, vendor, error description

**Sorting:** Sort by any column to prioritize errors by occurrence count, last seen time, or error type.

**Debugging delivery failures:**

1. **Filter by vendor** → Determine if errors are vendor-specific
2. **Filter by channel** → Check if errors are channel-specific (e.g., all email failures but SMS works)
3. **Check vendor status** → Visit the vendor's status page for reported outages or service disruptions
4. **Review error patterns** → Look for patterns in error messages to identify root causes
5. **Consult Error Guide** → See the [Error Guide](/docs/error-guides) for vendor-specific resolution

For detailed troubleshooting and resolution steps, see the [Error Guide](/docs/error-guides).

## Exporting data

Export CSVs for analysis outside SuprSend (Sheets/Excel/warehouse).

![Export analytics as CSV](/images/export.gif)

### How to export

1. Navigate to the Analytics tab you want to export
2. Click the 📥 icon in the top right of that section
3. Select your date range (relative or absolute)
4. Choose your export type (aggregate or raw user data)
5. Apply any filters you want to include
6. Download the CSV file(s)

### Export types

**Aggregate user data** — Summary statistics grouped by date and time (matches what you see in charts). Includes: Date/time buckets, total counts, percentage rates, grouped by workflow/channel/category/vendor/template. Use cases: Custom dashboards, trend analysis, high-level reporting.

**Raw user data** — Individual user-level records with timestamps for available events. Includes: User records, timestamps for each event, workflow/template/channel/vendor/category, user properties and metadata. Use cases: Detailed user behavior analysis, pattern identification, custom analytics, data warehousing.

### Export limits

**File size limit:** Up to 50 million rows per file

**Multiple files:** If your export exceeds 50M rows, SuprSend automatically splits it into multiple files at the graph level.

**Partition ID:** Each file includes a `partition_id` column (0, 1, 2...) to identify and merge files. The partition ID tells you the file number:
- **1 file** → `partition_id = 0`
- **2 files** → `partition_id = 0` (first file), `partition_id = 1` (second file)
- **3 files** → `partition_id = 0, 1, 2` (and so on)

**How to merge multiple files:**

1. Download all files from your export
2. Sort by `partition_id` to ensure correct order
3. Combine files in order (partition_id 0, then 1, then 2, etc.)
4. Remove duplicate headers (keep header only from first file)

## Frequently Asked Questions

<AccordionGroup>

  <Accordion title="How up-to-date is the data?">
    Analytics data updates in near real-time, usually within a few minutes of events occurring. **Timeline breakdown:**
    
    - **Last hour:** Data may still be updating (expect some variance)
    - **1-24 hours ago:** Most data is finalized
    - **48+ hours ago:** Data is final and won't change
    
    For immediate verification of a specific event, use [Logs](/docs/logging) instead.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What timezone is the data shown in?">
    Analytics automatically uses your browser's timezone by default. You can change the timezone in the top navbar if you need to view data in a different timezone.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How long is data kept?">
    Data retention depends on your SuprSend plan. Check the [pricing page](https://www.suprsend.com/pricing) for specific retention periods for your plan tier.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What are good benchmark metrics for my industry?">
    See the [Benchmarks](#channel-performance) section above for general industry benchmarks. **Important:** Benchmarks vary significantly by:
    
    - Industry and use case
    - Channel type
    - User base and demographics
    
    Focus on **trends over time** rather than absolute numbers—are your metrics improving or declining? Use benchmarks as a starting point, but optimize based on what works for your specific audience.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How often should I check analytics?">
    **Recommended frequency:**
    
    - **Daily:** Check [Errors](#errors) for delivery issues that need immediate attention
    - **Weekly:** Review engagement trends and channel performance to spot patterns
    - **Monthly:** Perform deep optimization analysis, compare workflows, and adjust strategies
    
    Adjust based on your notification volume and business needs.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why are seen/clicked metrics missing or showing 0%?">
    If you see "seen" or "clicked" metrics showing as 0% or missing for channels that support them, here are the most common causes:
    
    - **Tracking not configured:** Configure the webhook URL `https://hub.suprsend.com/webhook/*` in your vendor account. See [vendor integration docs](/docs/vendors) for step-by-step instructions
    - **Vendor not sending events:** Your vendor may not be configured to send engagement events to SuprSend
    - **Recent notifications:** Data may still be updating (wait 24-48 hours for engagement data to populate)
    - **Channel limitation:** Some channels (SMS, Slack, MS Teams) don't support seen/clicked metrics—this is expected
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="My opt-out rate is high. What should I do?">
    High opt-out rates usually indicate over-messaging or relevance issues. See the [Unsubscription trends](#unsubscription-trends) section for detailed investigation steps and the [Notification volume](#notification-volume) section for reducing frequency.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why do the numbers keep changing?">
    Analytics numbers change because vendors send status updates over time. This is normal behavior:
    
    - **Why it happens:** Vendors report delivery and engagement events asynchronously. International SMS vendors are often slower to report than email or push providers
    - **What to expect:**
      - **Last 24 hours:** Data may still be updating
      - **24-48 hours ago:** Data is mostly finalized
      - **48+ hours ago:** Data is final and won't change
    
    For immediate verification of a specific event, use [Logs](/docs/logging) instead of Analytics.
  </Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>
