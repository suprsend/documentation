Analytics provides visibility into your notification system. Track notification lifecycle signals, understand user engagement patterns, and optimize your workflows based on near real-time data.

Navigate to the [Analytics tab](https://app.suprsend.com/en/production/analytics/) to get started.

<Note>
  **Analytics vs Logs:** Analytics shows trends and summaries (like "email has 45% open rate"). [Logs](/docs/logging) shows individual events (like "this email was delivered at 2:34 PM"). Use Analytics to spot patterns, then use Logs to investigate specific issues.
</Note>

---

## Available data points

Analytics provides visibility into notification performance across multiple dimensions. You can analyze data by:

* **[Channel performance](#channel-performance)** — Compare delivery and engagement across channels
* **[Notification volume](#notification-volume)** — Monitor average notifications per user per day
* **[Unsubscription trends](#unsubscription-trends)** — Track opt-outs by channel, category, and time period
* **[Workflow Level Breakdown](#workflow-level-breakdown)** — Analyze workflows, templates, channels, and vendors
* **[API Request Failures](#api-request-failures)** — API or SDK request processing errors
* **[Workflow / Broadcast Execution Failures](#workflow--broadcast-execution-failures)** — Errors during workflow or broadcast execution
* **[Notification Delivery Failures](#notification-delivery-failures)** — Vendor-reported delivery issues

## Performance Overview

Performance Overview provides a high-level view of your notification system's health. Start here to assess overall performance, then explore specific areas in detail.

### Channel performance

Compare delivery and engagement across channels to see where users are most responsive. You can filter by template—in addition to the [global filters](#filters) (workflow, tenant, date range)—to compare performance for specific templates.

<Frame caption="Which channels are performing the best?">
  ![](/images/channel-perf.png)
</Frame>

The chart above shows these metrics by channel. Here's what each metric means and why it matters.

**Core metrics:**

| Metric | What it means | How it's computed | Why it matters |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Delivered** | Provider or channel reports a delivered (or final) status | Recorded when SuprSend receives a provider delivery status (or equivalent final status) | If delivery is low, notifications aren't reaching recipients reliably |
| **Seen** | A view/open signal is received (varies by channel) | Recorded when tracking events are received | Shows if your content is engaging enough to open |
| **Clicked** | User clicked on the notification | Tracked when users click links or buttons in notifications via click tracking URLs | Indicates if your call-to-action is effective |
| **Opt-out** | The user unsubscribed from notifications | Tracked when users unsubscribe via links, preferences, or API | High opt-outs mean users are annoyed or notifications aren't relevant |

<Note>
  **Seen ≠ engaged.** A notification can be seen without being clicked. Seen means opened/viewed; clicked means the user took action.
</Note>

**Channel support for metrics:**

Not all channels support all metrics. Here's what's available at the channel level:

| Channel | Delivered | Seen | Clicked |
|---------|-----------|------|---------|
| Email | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| SMS | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |
| WhatsApp | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ |
| Android Push | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| iOS Push | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Web Push | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Inbox | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Slack | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |
| MS Teams | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |

Not every channel supports seen or clicked tracking—SMS and Slack, for example, only report delivery. Keep that in mind when comparing engagement across channels.

<Note>
  Configure `https://hub.suprsend.com/webhook/*` in your vendor dashboard to track delivery, seen, and click events for email, SMS and WhatsApp. See [vendor integration docs](/docs/vendors) for step-by-step instructions.
</Note>

<Note>
  For Android push, "seen" also includes notifications cleared from the tray. Some channels (SMS, Slack) don't support engagement tracking.
</Note>

**Benchmarks:**

Use these benchmarks as a starting point—they vary by industry, use case, and user base. Focus on trends over time—are your metrics improving or declining?

Benchmarks vary widely by use case. Treat these as directional signals, not targets.

| Metric | ✅ Good | ⚠️ Warning | 🔴 Bad |
|--------|---------|------------|--------|
| **Email delivery rate** | 95%+ | 85–95% | Below 85% |
| **Email seen rate** | 20%+ | 10–20% | Below 10% |
| **WhatsApp delivery rate** | 95%+ | 85–95% | Below 85% |
| **WhatsApp seen rate** | 60%+ | 40–60% | Below 40% |
| **Push delivery rate** (Android, iOS, Web Push) | 70%+ | 50–70% | Below 50% |
| **Push seen rate** (Android, iOS, Web Push) | 30%+ | 15–30% | Below 15% |
| **Click rate** (Email, Push, Inbox) | 3%+ | 1–3% | Below 1% |
| **Opt-out rate** (all channels) | Below 0.1% | 0.1–0.5% | Above 0.5% |

Use these ranges as a starting point; they vary by industry and use case. If your numbers sit in the warning or bad range, the guidance below can help.


**Interpreting your metrics:**

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Low delivery rates" icon="triangle-exclamation" iconType="solid">
    Investigate vendor issues, invalid contact information, or delivery failures in [Errors](#errors).
  </Card>
  <Card title="Low engagement rates" icon="circle-question" iconType="solid">

  </Card>

Beyond single metrics, watch for these patterns over time—they often point to the next place to investigate.

**Key patterns to watch:**

- **Outlier channels** — Identify channels with consistently high or low performance. Use [Workflow Level Breakdown](#workflow-level-breakdown) to compare workflows and understand which specific workflows drive these patterns.
- **Time-based engagement patterns** — Engagement often varies by time of day or day of week. Use time-based filters to identify optimal send times and compare engagement rates across different periods.
- **Sudden delivery drops** — A sudden decrease in delivery rate indicates potential issues. Check [Errors](#errors) and see the [Error Guide](/docs/error-guides) for detailed troubleshooting.

### Notification volume

Monitor average notifications sent per user per day. **Over-messaging is the #1 reason users unsubscribe.**

<Note>
  **Requires notification categories:** Set up [notification categories](/docs/managing-notification-categories) to analyze volume by category.
</Note>

<Frame caption="Average notifications per user showing volume trends and correlation with opt-outs">
  ![](/images/avgnotif.png)
</Frame>

Use this view to spot over-messaging before opt-outs spike. The following breakdown explains what you're looking at.

**What you'll see:**

* **Average notifications per user** — Average number of notifications each user receives per day
* **Volume trends over time** — How notification volume changes over time
* **Volume by category** — Which notification categories contribute most to total volume
* **Volume by channel** — Which channels are sending the most notifications
* **Correlation with opt-outs** — Compare volume trends and opt-out trends side by side to check if spikes align

These ranges reflect typical consumer products. B2B, transactional, or ops-heavy products may differ.

| Status | Volume | What it means |
|--------|--------|--------------|
| ✅ **Good** | 2-3 notifications per user per day | Users are engaged without being overwhelmed |
| ⚠️ **Warning** | 4-9 per day | Monitor opt-out rates closely |
| 🔴 **Red zone** | 10+ per day | Users will likely unsubscribe. Take action immediately. |

If volume sits in the warning or red zone, work through the steps below to find the cause.

<Note>
  These ranges are general guidelines. Transaction-heavy products may legitimately exceed them. Monitor your opt-out rates to determine what works for your users.
</Note>

**Investigation steps:**

1. Check if notification volume trends correlate with rising opt-outs
2. Filter by category to identify which categories send the most notifications
3. Use [Workflow Level Breakdown](#workflow-level-breakdown)—sort by trigger count to find high-volume workflows
4. Review timing patterns—are multiple notifications being sent simultaneously?

See [Unsubscription trends](#unsubscription-trends) for detailed opt-out investigation steps.

**Reducing volume:**

- Use [throttling](/docs/throttle) to limit how often workflows trigger per user
- Use [batch](/docs/batch) to consolidate multiple updates into single notifications
- Review workflow triggers—ensure conditions aren't too broad
- Prioritize notifications—send critical ones immediately and batch others

### Unsubscription trends

Monitor opt-outs by channel, category, and time period to understand why users unsubscribe.

<Note>
  **User preferences required:** This section is available when you've enabled SuprSend's [preference center](/docs/user-preferences). Without preferences, opt-outs may be limited or unavailable. With preferences enabled, you can track opt-outs by category, channel, and time period to identify patterns and understand why users unsubscribe.
</Note>

<Frame caption="Unsubscription trends showing opt-out patterns, day-wise breakdown, and category/channel analysis">
  ![](/images/optout.png)
</Frame>

This view shows where and when users opt out—by channel, category, and time. Use it to connect opt-outs to volume or specific campaigns.

**What you'll see:**

* **Total opt-outs** — Number and percentage of users who unsubscribed
* **Day-wise trends** — Opt-out patterns over time (use available time breakdowns to spot spikes)
* **Category breakdown** — Which categories drive the most opt-outs
* **Channel breakdown** — Which channels contribute most to opt-outs
* **Opt-out rate** — Percentage of users who unsubscribed
* **Correlation with volume** — Compare opt-out trends and volume trends side by side to check if spikes align

**Warning signs:**

<Warning>
  Watch for: Opt-out rate above 0.5%, spikes in opt-outs for specific categories, high opt-outs from a single channel, or opt-out rates increasing over time
</Warning>

When you spot warning signs, follow these steps to get to the cause.

**Investigation steps:**

1. **Check notification volume first** — Over-messaging is the most common cause. Check if volume trends correlate with opt-out spikes.
2. **Filter by category** — Identify which categories have the highest opt-out rates.
3. **Filter by channel** — Determine which channels are driving opt-outs.
4. **Analyze day-wise trends** — Check if opt-outs spiked after a specific campaign or change.
5. **Review content relevance** — Assess whether notifications are valuable and relevant to users.

These common patterns help you interpret what you're seeing and decide what to change.

## Workflow Level Breakdown

The Workflow Level Breakdown helps you compare notification performance **at the most actionable level** — individual workflows, templates, channels, and vendors.

Before diving into individual workflows, use Performance Overview above to check overall health and spot obvious red flags. Then use this view to drill into specific workflows, templates, and channels.

<Frame caption="Workflow Level Breakdown view">
  ![](/images/wf-level.png)
</Frame>

### What each row represents

Each row represents a **unique delivery path**, defined by the combination of:

* **Workflow**
* **Template**
* **Channel**
* **Vendor**
* **Category**

For example, the same workflow may appear multiple times if it sends notifications via different channels or vendors.

This allows you to compare how the *same notification logic* performs across delivery paths.

The columns in the table map to the metrics below.

### Metrics shown

| Column                    | Meaning                                                                               |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Triggered**             | Number of times this delivery path was triggered in the selected time range           |
| **% Delivered**           | Percentage of triggered notifications that reached the vendor or final delivery state |
| **% Seen / Delivered**    | Percentage of delivered notifications that were opened or viewed (where supported)    |
| **% Clicked / Delivered** | Percentage of delivered notifications that received a click (where supported)         |

<Note>
Seen and clicked metrics depend on channel capabilities and tracking setup. Some channels (SMS, Slack) do not support engagement tracking.
</Note>

The **Total** row at the bottom aggregates metrics across all rows in the current view and respects applied filters.

Use the filters and sort options below to narrow in on the workflows or delivery paths that matter most.

### Filters and sorting

**Filters:**

* **Workflow** — Compare multiple workflows side by side
* **Channel** — Evaluate which channels perform best
* **Vendor** — Compare vendor performance (e.g. SendGrid vs others)
* **Category** — Analyze performance by notification intent
* **Template** — Identify templates driving low engagement
* **Interaction status** — Filter by "good interactions" (high engagement) or "bad interactions" (low engagement)

**Sorting:** Click any column header to sort. Sort by lowest seen/click rate to find underperformers, highest to find top performers, or trigger count for high-volume workflows.

Use filters in combination to answer focused questions, such as:

> "How do transactional emails sent via SendGrid perform compared to inbox notifications?"

### Analysis strategies

#### Identify top performers

* Sort by **% Seen / Delivered** or **% Clicked / Delivered** (descending)
* Look for patterns in channel, timing, or template design
* Reuse successful patterns across similar workflows

#### Find underperforming notifications

* Sort by **lowest engagement rates**
* Compare against similar workflows in the same category
* Check whether the issue is content, channel choice, or delivery path

#### Compare channels for the same workflow

If the same workflow appears for multiple channels:

* Compare engagement rates side by side
* Decide whether certain channels should be deprioritized or removed
* Consider using [smart delivery](/docs/smart-delivery) to automatically choose the best channel

#### Diagnose delivery problems

* Low **% Delivered** indicates delivery or vendor issues
* Click through to the [Errors](#errors) tab to investigate root causes
* Validate contact data and vendor configuration

## Errors

When Analytics shows delivery drops or missing notifications, the Errors tab helps you identify why.

The Errors tab helps you identify and resolve issues that prevent notifications from being triggered, executed, or delivered. Unlike Logs (which show every event), Errors aggregates failures so you can quickly spot patterns and prioritize fixes.

---

### Error types

Errors are grouped into three sections, based on **where the failure occurred** in the notification lifecycle:

1. **API Request Failures** — Requests that never started a workflow
2. **Workflow / Broadcast Execution Failures** — Errors while running workflows or rendering templates
3. **Notification Delivery Failures** — Vendor-reported delivery issues after sending

Use filters and sorting to narrow down issues by workflow, channel, vendor, or time range. The sections below walk through each error type and how to debug them.

### API Request Failures

Errors where API or SDK requests were not successfully processed, and **no workflow execution was started**.

<Frame caption="API Request Failures">
  ![](/images/api-req-err.png)
</Frame>

If this error table is empty and you're actively sending requests, your integration is working as expected.

**What you'll see:**

* **Total occurrences** — How often the error occurred in the selected time range
* **Last seen** — When the error last happened (to see if it's ongoing)
* **Context** — API type or request context involved
* **Error description** — The exact error message reported

**Common causes:**

* Invalid or expired API key
* Authentication failure
* Missing required fields (e.g. `user_id`)
* Invalid request payload format

**Filters:** API type, error description. **Sortable.**

---

### Workflow / Broadcast Execution Failures

Errors that occur **after a request is accepted**, but while executing a workflow or broadcast.
These usually indicate issues with workflow configuration or template data.

<Frame caption="Workflow / Broadcast Execution Failures">
  ![](/images/wf-fail.png)
</Frame>

Each row represents a workflow (or broadcast) and the node where execution failed.

**What you'll see:**

* **Total occurrences** — How often the error occurred in the selected time range
* **Last seen** — When the error last happened (to see if it's ongoing)
* **Workflow** — The workflow where the failure occurred
* **Node Name** — The specific node in the workflow where execution failed
* **Error description** — The exact error message reported

**Most common errors:**

* Template rendering failures due to missing variables
* Invalid data types passed into templates or nodes
* Misconfigured workflow logic

**Example errors:**

```
email: template rendering failed. error: "actor_name" not defined
email: template rendering failed. error: "code" not defined
```

**How to debug:**

1. Check the **Node Name** column to see where the failure occurred
2. Read the error description—it usually names the missing or invalid variable
3. Review the workflow and template configuration
4. Verify the data payload sent to the workflow

If the same error appears multiple times with a high occurrence count, it indicates a systemic issue affecting many notifications.

**Filters:** Node name, error description. **Sortable.**

### Notification Delivery Failures

Errors reported by notification vendors **after SuprSend successfully sent the request**, but the vendor could not deliver the notification.

<Frame caption="Notification Delivery Failures">
  ![](/images/notif-deliv-fail.png)
</Frame>

**What you'll see:**

* **Total occurrences** — How often the error occurred in the selected time range
* **Last seen** — When the error last happened (to see if it's ongoing)
* **Template** — The template slug for the failed notification
* **Channel** — The channel where delivery failed (email, SMS, push, etc.)
* **Vendor** — The vendor that reported the delivery failure
* **Error description** — The exact error message reported

**Common delivery errors:**

* Invalid or non-existent email address or phone number
* Mailbox unavailable or permanently blocked
* Vendor-side rate limiting
* Temporary vendor outages

**Example errors:**

```
550 Requested action not taken: mailbox unavailable
550 5.1.1 The email account does not exist
Invalid phone number
```

**How to debug:**

1. Filter by **vendor** to check if the issue is vendor-specific
2. Filter by **channel** to isolate delivery issues (email vs SMS vs push)
3. Check the vendor's status page for outages
4. Review user contact data for invalid or outdated values

Some delivery failures (like invalid addresses) require fixing user data. Others (like temporary outages) may resolve automatically.

**Filters:** Template slug, channel, vendor, error description. **Sortable.**

If errors persist or you're unsure how to resolve a specific failure, see the [Error Guide](/docs/error-guides) or contact support.

## Exporting data

Export CSVs for analysis outside SuprSend (Sheets/Excel/warehouse).

<Note>
  **TL;DR:** Small exports = single CSV. Large exports = multiple files with `partition_id`. Merge in order.
</Note>

### How to export

<Frame caption="Exporting analytics data">
  ![](/images/export.gif)
</Frame>

1. Navigate to the Analytics tab you want to export
2. Click the 📥 icon in the top right of that section
3. Select your date range (relative or absolute)
4. Choose your export type (aggregate or raw user data)
5. Apply any filters you want to include
6. Download the CSV file(s)

### Export types

**Aggregate user data** — Summary statistics grouped by date and time (matches what you see in charts). Includes: Date/time buckets, total counts, percentage rates, grouped by workflow/channel/category/vendor/template. Use cases: Custom dashboards, trend analysis, high-level reporting.

**Raw user data** — Individual user-level records with timestamps for available events. Includes: User records, timestamps for each event, workflow/template/channel/vendor/category, user properties and metadata. Use cases: Detailed user behavior analysis, pattern identification, custom analytics, data warehousing.

### Export limits

**File size limit:** Up to 50 million rows per file

**Multiple files:** If your export exceeds 50M rows, SuprSend automatically splits it into multiple files at the graph level.

**Partition ID:** Each file includes a `partition_id` column (0, 1, 2...) to identify and merge files. The partition ID tells you the file number:

* **1 file** → `partition_id = 0`
* **2 files** → `partition_id = 0` (first file), `partition_id = 1` (second file)
* **3 files** → `partition_id = 0, 1, 2` (and so on)

**How to merge multiple files:**

1. Download all files from your export
2. Sort by `partition_id` to ensure correct order
3. Combine files in order (partition_id 0, then 1, then 2, etc.)
4. Remove duplicate headers (keep header only from first file)

## Navigating Analytics

### Refresh

Analytics data updates automatically. You can manually refresh the data by clicking the refresh button to get the latest metrics.

### Filters

You have global filters to drill down analytics data by workflow, tenant, and date range.

**Global filters:**

* **Workflow** — Filter analytics by specific workflows
* **Tenant** — Filter analytics by tenant (for multi-tenant setups)

### Date Range

Analytics default to "Last 7 days" when first opened. Choose from relative ranges (Last 1 day, 7 days, 30 days, and additional presets) or select absolute date ranges with timezone-aware selection.

Older data is retained for a period of time based on your [pricing plan](https://www.suprsend.com/pricing).

For extended retention options, contact our [sales team](mailto:sales@suprsend.com).

## Frequently Asked Questions

<AccordionGroup>

  <Accordion title="How up-to-date is the data?">
    Analytics data updates in near real-time, usually within a few minutes of events occurring. **Timeline breakdown:**
    
    - **Last hour:** Data may still be updating (expect some variance)
    - **1-24 hours ago:** Most data is finalized
    - **48+ hours ago:** Data is final and won't change
    
    For immediate verification of a specific event, use [Logs](/docs/logging) instead.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What timezone is the data shown in?">
    Analytics automatically uses your browser's timezone by default. You can change the timezone in the top navbar if you need to view data in a different timezone.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How long is data kept?">
    Data retention depends on your SuprSend plan. Check the [pricing page](https://www.suprsend.com/pricing) for specific retention periods for your plan tier.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What are good benchmark metrics for my industry?">
    See the [Benchmarks](#channel-performance) section above for general industry benchmarks. **Important:** Benchmarks vary significantly by:
    
    - Industry and use case
    - Channel type
    - User base and demographics
    
    Focus on **trends over time** rather than absolute numbers—are your metrics improving or declining? Use benchmarks as a starting point, but optimize based on what works for your specific audience.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How often should I check analytics?">
    **Recommended frequency:**
    
    - **Daily:** Check [Errors](#errors) for delivery issues that need immediate attention
    - **Weekly:** Review engagement trends and channel performance to spot patterns
    - **Monthly:** Perform deep optimization analysis, compare workflows, and adjust strategies
    
    Adjust based on your notification volume and business needs.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why are seen/clicked metrics missing or showing 0%?">
    If you see "seen" or "clicked" metrics showing as 0% or missing for channels that support them, here are the most common causes:
    
    - **Tracking not configured:** Configure the webhook URL `https://hub.suprsend.com/webhook/*` in your vendor account. See [vendor integration docs](/docs/vendors) for step-by-step instructions
    - **Vendor not sending events:** Your vendor may not be configured to send engagement events to SuprSend
    - **Recent notifications:** Data may still be updating (wait 24-48 hours for engagement data to populate)
    - **Channel limitation:** Some channels (SMS, Slack, MS Teams) don't support seen/clicked metrics—this is expected
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="My opt-out rate is high. What should I do?">
    High opt-out rates usually indicate over-messaging or relevance issues. See the [Unsubscription trends](#unsubscription-trends) section for detailed investigation steps and the [Notification volume](#notification-volume) section for reducing frequency.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why do the numbers keep changing?">
    Analytics numbers change because vendors send status updates over time. This is normal behavior:
    
    - **Why it happens:** Vendors report delivery and engagement events asynchronously. International SMS vendors are often slower to report than email or push providers
    - **What to expect:**
      - **Last 24 hours:** Data may still be updating
      - **24-48 hours ago:** Data is mostly finalized
      - **48+ hours ago:** Data is final and won't change
    
    For immediate verification of a specific event, use [Logs](/docs/logging) instead of Analytics.
  </Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>
