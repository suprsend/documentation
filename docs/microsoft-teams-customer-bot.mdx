title: "Send DMs or post in channels via your customer's Teams app"

---

This page covers the case where the Teams app and bot belong to **the customer**, not you. Everything else — objects for channels, users for DMs, the trigger — is the same as [Send via your Teams app](/docs/microsoft-teams-customer-workspaces). Only the credentials change at send time.

Let's say you sell to a hospital network or a bank. Their security team won't approve a third-party bot in their Entra directory. Instead, they register *their own* Teams app in *their* directory, hand you its credentials, and expect every Teams message for their account to come from that app.

## Key concepts

| In your product | In SuprSend | Example |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Customer organization** | **[Tenant](/docs/tenants)**, required here — it's what the customer's bot credentials attach to. Pass `tenant_id` on every trigger for that customer. | `tenant_id: "customer-a"` |
| **Customer's bot credentials** | **[Tenant vendor](/docs/tenant-vendor)** for Microsoft Teams on that tenant. Overrides your default vendor for this customer's sends. | App ID, secret, App Type, Tenant ID |
| **Monitored service** (any resource that owns a channel) | **[Object](/docs/objects)** with an `$ms_teams` entry holding the channel's `conversation_id`. | `services` / `api-prod` → `#incidents` |
| **Person to DM** | **[User](/docs/users)** with an `$ms_teams` entry holding their `user_id`, on a [per-tenant profile](/docs/user-tenant-mapping). | `distinct_id: "on-call-engineer"` |
| **Customer's Microsoft Entra directory** | `tenant_id` inside each `$ms_teams` entry — optional here. Sends for this tenant already use *their* vendor, whose home Tenant ID is their directory, so SuprSend falls back to that if you leave it out. | `c1981ab2-9aaf-4f36-8c1e-7d2a54e0b3f9` |

## 1. The customer creates the Teams app

Point their IT team at [Create a Teams app](/docs/microsoft-teams-create-app). They run those steps in their own Azure and Teams Developer Portal, with three differences from your own setup:

- **Supported account types** can stay single-directory (`AzureADMyOrg`). This bot never leaves their tenant.
- Azure Bot **App Type** is still **Single tenant**.
- They send you the **Application (client) ID**, **client secret**, and **Directory (tenant) ID**. The app package stays with them — their admins install it from their own org catalog.

If they'd rather you provision it, the [Azure CLI script](/docs/microsoft-teams-create-app#alternative-create-the-bot-with-the-azure-cli) on that page works in a subscription they grant you access to; use `--sign-in-audience AzureADMyOrg`.

## 2. The customer installs the app in their tenant

Their admin uploads the app package in **Teams admin center → Teams apps → Manage apps → Upload new app**, then gives it permission to send:

- **DMs** — add the app to the *Global* setup policy under **Teams apps → Setup policies → Installed apps**, so it's installed in personal scope for everyone.
- **Channel posts** — @mention the bot once in each channel you'll post in, which installs the app in that team.

This is the same as Option A on [Send via your Teams app](/docs/microsoft-teams-customer-workspaces#give-the-app-permission-to-send-dms-and-post-in-channels). Because the app is theirs and lives in their catalog, there's no Store listing or `.zip` from your side.

## 3. Save their credentials on a tenant vendor

Create the SuprSend tenant for this customer if it doesn't exist:

```bash
curl -X POST "https://hub.suprsend.com/v1/tenant/customer-a/" \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer __API_KEY__" \
  --header "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --data '{ "tenant_name": "Customer A" }'
```

Then open **Vendors → Microsoft Teams** with **that tenant selected** in the tenant switcher ([open vendors](https://app.suprsend.com/en/staging/vendors/)) and fill in *their* values:

<Frame>
  <img src="/images/docs/msteams-suprsend-vendor-page.png" alt="SuprSend Microsoft Teams vendor form with App Type and Tenant ID" />
</Frame>

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Application (client) ID** | Their bot's App ID |
| **Password** | Their client secret |
| **App Type** | Matches **their** Azure Bot — `Single tenant` for new bots |
| **Tenant ID** | **Their** Directory (tenant) ID |

After you save, SuprSend logs in with these credentials and shows the result in the **Access Token** field. Empty means the login failed — usually a wrong Tenant ID. Filled means it worked, but a mismatched App Type can still fail every send with `401`, so send a test before going further.

<Note>
  Your default (workspace) vendor stays as *your* bot for customers who install your app. Only customers who bring their own app get a tenant vendor. Passing `tenant_id: "customer-a"` on a trigger is what selects their vendor.
</Note>

There isn't a public API for the vendor form today; set it in the dashboard. Tenant-vendor API access is in beta — email [support@suprsend.com](mailto:support@suprsend.com) if you need it.

## 4. Save Teams channels and users in SuprSend

Channels go on the service object, DMs on the user's per-tenant profile — the same as the your-app path. Collecting the IDs works the same way too; see [In production, fetch the IDs automatically with Graph](/docs/microsoft-teams-customer-workspaces#in-production-fetch-the-ids-automatically-with-graph).

<CodeGroup>
```bash Channel on object
curl -X POST "https://hub.suprsend.com/v1/object/services/api-prod/" \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer __API_KEY__" \
  --header "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --data '{
    "name": "API production",
    "$ms_teams": [{
      "tenant_id": "c1981ab2-9aaf-4f36-8c1e-7d2a54e0b3f9",
      "conversation_id": "19:abc123def@thread.tacv2"
    }]
  }'
```

```bash DM on user (per-tenant)
curl -X POST "https://hub.suprsend.com/v1/user/on-call-engineer/tenant/customer-a/" \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer __API_KEY__" \
  --header "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --data '{
    "$ms_teams": [{
      "tenant_id": "c1981ab2-9aaf-4f36-8c1e-7d2a54e0b3f9",
      "user_id": "3fa85f64-5717-4562-b3fc-2c963f66afa6"
    }]
  }'
```
</CodeGroup>

`tenant_id` on the entry is optional on this path, because the tenant vendor already knows their directory. Including it is harmless and keeps the entries identical to the your-app path.

Don't put `incoming_webhook` on these recipients. Webhooks ignore tenant vendors and fire for every URL on the profile.

## 5. Trigger a workflow

Create a workflow (for example `new-incident`) with a **multi-channel** or **MS Teams** node connected to a [Teams template](/docs/ms-teams-template). Trigger it with the service object and the on-call user as recipients, and **always** pass the customer's `tenant_id` — that's what routes the send through their vendor:

```json
{
  "workflow": "new-incident",
  "tenant_id": "customer-a",
  "recipients": [
    { "object_type": "services", "id": "api-prod" },
    "on-call-engineer"
  ],
  "data": { "severity": "high", "summary": "API p99 > 2s" }
}
```

SuprSend authenticates as the customer's bot, posts to the channel on `api-prod`, and DMs the on-call engineer from their per-tenant `$ms_teams` entry.

<Check>
  If everything works, the channel post and the DM arrive from **the customer's** app name, not yours. You can also check **Logs → Messages** in SuprSend for the delivery and later statuses — the tenant column shows which customer's vendor was used.
</Check>

## FAQs

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I have some customers on my app and others on their own?">
    Yes. Customers who install your app use the default vendor; customers who bring their own app get a tenant vendor. The trigger's `tenant_id` decides which vendor a send uses — the same workflow and templates serve both.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Posts still come from my bot, not the customer's">
    The trigger didn't pass `tenant_id`, or that tenant has no Microsoft Teams vendor. Without a tenant vendor, SuprSend uses the default workspace vendor — yours.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Sends fail with 401 Authorization has been denied for this request">
    **App Type** on their tenant vendor doesn't match their Azure Bot. Bots created after 31 July 2025 are single-tenant.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Access Token is empty with AADSTS700016">
    **Tenant ID** on the vendor form isn't the directory where they registered the bot. Use their Directory (tenant) ID.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What does BotNotInConversationRoster mean?">
    The app is in their tenant but not installed in the team that owns that channel. Their admin @mentions the bot once in the channel, or installs the app in the team.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
